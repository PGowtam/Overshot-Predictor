2.3 ICT Smart Money Theory — Core Concepts
The ICT methodology, developed by Michael J. Huddleston, teaches traders to read markets through the lens of institutional liquidity rather than conventional technical analysis. Institutions (banks, hedge funds, central banks) move such large volumes that their orders cannot be filled at a single price. They must accumulate and distribute positions across price ranges, and they do so by engineered price moves that trigger retail stop orders, providing the liquidity they need.

2.3.1 Order Blocks (OBs)
An Order Block is the last significant bullish or bearish candle directly preceding a major impulsive price move. It represents the zone where institutional orders were placed. Price frequently returns to these zones to mitigate (partially fill) the original orders before continuing in the original direction.
	•	Bullish Order Block: Last bearish (red) candle before a significant upward impulse
	•	Bearish Order Block: Last bullish (green) candle before a significant downward impulse
	•	Mitigation: When price returns to the OB zone, this is the entry trigger
	•	Validity: OBs remain valid until fully mitigated or broken by a structural shift

2.3.2 Fair Value Gaps (FVGs)
A Fair Value Gap is a three-candle formation where the third candle's body does not overlap with the first candle's body, creating a price imbalance (gap) that algorithms tend to fill. FVGs represent areas where price moved so aggressively that only one side of the market participated.
	•	Identification: Candle 1 high < Candle 3 low (bullish FVG) or Candle 1 low > Candle 3 high (bearish FVG)
	•	Entry: Limit orders placed at the 50% (equilibrium) of the FVG for optimal fill
	•	Fill priority: FVGs are typically mitigated before major directional continuation
	•	Partial fills: Price need not fill the entire FVG; a 50% mitigation is sufficient confirmation

2.3.3 Liquidity Pools
Liquidity pools are price levels where a significant cluster of stop-loss orders from retail traders accumulate. Institutions hunt these pools to fill their own large orders. Common liquidity pools in XAUUSD include:
	•	Equal highs and equal lows — double/triple tops or bottoms on any timeframe
	•	Previous Day High (PDH) and Previous Day Low (PDL)
	•	Previous Week High (PWH) and Previous Week Low (PWL)
	•	Asian session high and Asian session low (the most frequently swept daily)
	•	Round numbers — $2,000, $2,100, $2,500, $3,000, etc. (psychological magnets)
	•	Trend line clusters — where retail traders place stops below/above trend lines

2.3.4 Market Structure — BOS and CHoCH
Market structure defines the directional bias at each timeframe. Two events mark structure transitions:
	•	Break of Structure (BOS): Price breaks beyond the previous swing high (bullish continuation) or swing low (bearish continuation). Confirms existing trend direction.
	•	Change of Character (CHoCH): Price breaks against the prevailing structure — the first sign that the institutional bias has shifted. This is the entry signal in the 4-step model.
	•	Macro to micro flow: W1 > D1 > H4 > H1 > M15 > M5. Higher timeframe structure always takes precedence.

2.3.5 Premium and Discount Arrays
In any price range (swing low to swing high), the upper 50% is the Premium zone and the lower 50% is the Discount zone. Institutions buy in discount and sell in premium.
	•	Bullish bias: Only seek long entries when price retraces into the 50–79% discount zone
	•	Bearish bias: Only seek short entries when price rallies into the 50–79% premium zone
	•	Optimal Trade Entry (OTE): The 62–79% Fibonacci retracement of any swing range is the highest-probability entry zone

4. The 4-Confirmation Entry Model
Every trade taken under this system must satisfy all four confirmation gates sequentially. Satisfying three of four is insufficient — skip the trade. The gates are ordered temporally: you cannot reach Gate C without Gate B having fired first.

4.1 Gate A — Liquidity Sweep
A liquidity sweep is the foundational trigger. It represents institutional order filling — the market maker reaching above or below a visible liquidity pool to collect stop orders before reversing.
Criteria for a Valid Sweep:
	•	A wick (shadow) on a candle extends beyond an identified liquidity pool (ASH, ASL, PDH, PDL, equal highs/lows, round numbers)
	•	The candle CLOSES back below the swept high (for a bearish sweep) or above the swept low (for a bullish sweep)
	•	The sweep occurs within a designated kill zone window
	•	The sweep is against the higher-timeframe order flow direction (i.e., a brief move against trend before continuation)
Invalidation:
	•	The candle closes beyond the swept level (this is a genuine breakout, not a sweep)
	•	The sweep occurs outside the kill zone window — wait for the next valid window
	•	Volume is abnormally low during the sweep — insufficient institutional participation

4.2 Gate B — Change of Character (CHoCH)
Following a confirmed liquidity sweep, the CHoCH is the first structural evidence that price direction has reversed at the institutional level.
Criteria for a Valid CHoCH:
	•	On M15 or M5, price forms a swing low (in bullish setup) or swing high (in bearish setup) after the sweep
	•	Price then breaks and closes ABOVE the previous swing high (bullish CHoCH) or BELOW the previous swing low (bearish CHoCH)
	•	The break must occur on a displacement candle — a candle with a large body relative to recent average candle size
	•	The CHoCH must occur on M5 or M15 — higher timeframe CHoCH is less precise for entry timing
Invalidation:
	•	Price breaks structure but immediately reverses — this is a false CHoCH, often another liquidity grab
	•	The CHoCH occurs more than 30 minutes after the original sweep — timing relevance has expired

4.3 Gate C — Order Block or FVG Re-test
After the CHoCH, price will typically retrace into the institutional zone that caused the structural shift. This retracement is the entry trigger.
Order Block Entry Protocol:
	•	Identify the last bearish candle (for longs) or last bullish candle (for shorts) before the CHoCH impulse
	•	This candle's body forms the order block zone (high to low of the candle body, not the wick)
	•	Place a limit order at the 50% mark of the OB body (the mitigation level)
	•	Alternatively, enter on the first M1 candle that closes inside the OB after the retracement enters it
Fair Value Gap Entry Protocol:
	•	If a 3-candle FVG formed during the CHoCH impulse, enter at the 50% equilibrium of the FVG
	•	Place limit order at FVG midpoint — this is the Optimal Trade Entry (OTE)
	•	If price passes through the FVG without triggering, re-evaluate using the next OB level
Confluence Priority (highest to lowest):
	•	OB + FVG overlap: Highest priority — both point to same price zone
	•	OB only at HTF discount/premium zone: High priority
	•	FVG only with HTF alignment: Standard priority
	•	No OB or FVG but strong CHoCH: Reduce position size by 50%

4.4 Gate D — Displacement Candle Confirmation (Optional but Recommended)
The displacement candle is the optional fourth gate — a strong, large-bodied candle from the OB/FVG zone confirming institutional participation at the entry level. Using this gate reduces win rate but significantly improves trade quality.
	•	The displacement candle must close beyond the OB/FVG zone midpoint in the trade direction
	•	Body must represent at least 60% of the total candle range (high to low)
	•	Volume on the displacement candle should be above the 20-candle average (where volume data is available)
	•	If the displacement candle is absent, the setup is still valid — consider it at 75% confidence vs 90%+

5. Risk Management System
Risk management is the most critical component of long-term profitability. A high win rate strategy with poor risk management will fail. Every parameter below is a hard rule — not a guideline.

5.1 Position Sizing Formula
Position size is determined by the dollar amount at risk, the pip distance to the stop loss, and the pip value of the instrument. This formula must be calculated before every single trade.
POSITION SIZE FORMULA
Position Size (lots) = Account Risk ($) ÷ (Stop Loss in pips × Pip Value per lot)

Account Risk ($) = Account Balance × Risk Percentage (e.g., 1%)
Pip Value for XAUUSD standard lot (1.0): approximately $10 per pip at $2,000/oz gold price

Example: $10,000 account, 1% risk, 25-pip stop loss:
  Account Risk = $10,000 × 0.01 = $100
  Position Size = $100 ÷ (25 × $10) = 0.40 lots

6. Multi-Timeframe Analysis Framework
6.1 Top-Down Analysis Protocol
Every trade begins at the highest applicable timeframe and works down to the execution timeframe. This top-down process is executed once before the London session and once before the New York session each day.

6.1.1 Weekly Chart (W1) — Directional Macro Bias
	•	Identify the current major swing structure: Is price making higher highs and higher lows (bullish) or lower highs and lower lows (bearish)?
	•	Mark the most significant W1 order blocks above and below current price
	•	Mark the W1 premium zone (upper 50% of the weekly range) and discount zone (lower 50%)
	•	Note: Gold above its 20-week EMA with weekly closes above = structural bullish bias
	•	Output: A single word — BULLISH, BEARISH, or NEUTRAL

6.1.2 Daily Chart (D1) — Institutional Reference Points
	•	Identify and mark the most recent significant D1 order blocks (last 10 trading days)
	•	Mark all unmitigated (unfilled) D1 Fair Value Gaps
	•	Note the Previous Day High (PDH) and Previous Day Low (PDL) — these are primary daily liquidity targets
	•	Identify whether price is approaching a D1 premium or discount zone
	•	Cross-check: D1 bias must not conflict with W1 bias. If it does, do not trade until alignment restores.
	•	Output: Specific price zones where high-probability setups are expected

6.1.3 H4 Chart — Refine Area of Interest
	•	Map the H4 market structure: identify all recent BOS and CHoCH points
	•	Mark H4 OBs and FVGs that sit within the D1 area of interest
	•	These H4 zones narrow the hunt zone from a 100+ pip D1 range to a 20–40 pip window
	•	The H4 analysis confirms the direction and narrows the entry zone simultaneously

6.1.4 H1 Chart — Session Context
	•	Map H1 structure within the H4 context
	•	Mark H1 OBs, FVGs, and liquidity pools
	•	Note the Asian range on the H1 chart for the current day
	•	H1 provides the first level at which the liquidity sweep becomes visible

6.1.5 M15 / M5 Charts — Execution Timeframe
	•	M15: Gate B (CHoCH) and Gate C (OB/FVG entry) are primarily identified here
	•	M5: Gate D (displacement candle) confirmation and precise entry/exit timing
	•	The entry price, stop loss, and take profit levels are all set from M15/M5 analysis
	•	M1: Used only for entry precision timing (watching for the first displacement candle on M1 inside the OB zone)

TIMEFRAME ALIGNMENT RULE
W1 → D1 → H4 → H1 must all point to the same directional bias before executing a trade.
If three of four timeframes are aligned but one conflicts, reduce position size by 50%.
If fewer than three timeframes align, skip the trade regardless of how perfect the M15 setup appears.

9. Trade Setup Playbook — Specific Patterns
9.1 Setup Type A: London Asian Range Sweep
The highest-probability daily setup. Occurs when the London session sweeps the Asian range and reverses.
	•	Pre-market: Mark ASH and ASL. Determine H4/D1 bias direction.
	•	At 02:00 EST, monitor for price pushing toward the relevant liquidity level.
	•	Wait for sweep candle — wick extends beyond ASH or ASL, candle closes back inside range.
	•	On M15, identify the CHoCH that follows the sweep. Gate B confirmed.
	•	Identify OB or FVG formed during the CHoCH impulse. Place limit order.
	•	Set stop below sweep wick extreme + 7 pips. Set TP1 at 1:1, TP2 at next HTF level.
Historical win rate: ~80% when H4 and D1 bias align with trade direction.

9.2 Setup Type B: New York Reversal of London Move
When London makes a directional move, New York frequently reverses it (creating the classic 'London sweep, NY reversal' pattern). This is a counter-London setup but with-HTF-bias trade.
	•	At 07:00 EST, assess whether the London move was impulsive or corrective relative to D1 structure.
	•	If London moved into a D1/H4 OB or premium/discount extreme, anticipate NY reversal.
	•	Wait for the NY liquidity sweep of the London move's high or low.
	•	CHoCH on M15 in the reversal direction confirms the NY institutional shift.
	•	Enter on OB/FVG retest. TP targeting the London session open price or HTF OB.
Historical win rate: ~75% when London move was against D1 bias.

9.3 Setup Type C: ICT Silver Bullet (Time-Specific)
The highest time-precision setup in the system. Requires strict time-window discipline.
	•	Window: 03:00–04:00 EST (London Silver Bullet) or 10:00–11:00 EST (NY Silver Bullet).
	•	Within the window: Identify a 3-candle FVG on M5 following a liquidity sweep.
	•	Enter at the 50% equilibrium of the FVG with a limit order.
	•	Stop: Below the FVG low (for longs) + 5 pips. TP: 2:1 minimum.
	•	If no valid FVG forms within the 60-minute window, do not trade that session.
Historical win rate: ~82% within the time window with FVG confirmation. Drops to ~55% outside the window — never extend the window.

9.4 Setup Type D: News Event Post-Spike Structure Play
High-impact news events (NFP, CPI, FOMC) create massive price dislocations that leave large FVGs and OBs. These are traded 15–30 minutes AFTER the release, never during.
	•	Do not hold existing positions through the release. Exit or reduce to 25% size.
	•	After the spike, wait for price to find a local structure high/low on M5.
	•	Identify the FVG created by the spike — these are typically 30–100+ pips wide.
	•	On first retest of the FVG 50% level, enter with a tight stop (2–3 pips beyond the FVG).
	•	Target: Full FVG fill, then HTF structure levels beyond.
Historical win rate: ~70% (lower due to volatility and spread widening during news).

15. Psychological Framework & Discipline Protocol
15.1 The Psychology of Rule-Based Trading
The greatest threat to this system is not market conditions — it is the trader's own mind. Emotional decision-making (fear, greed, revenge trading, FOMO) is the primary cause of account loss even among traders with proven edge.

15.2 The Pre-Trade Mental Checklist
Before placing any trade, ask these questions aloud or in writing:
	•	Have I satisfied all 4 entry gates in sequence?
	•	Is my stop loss at a logical structural invalidation point?
	•	Am I risking no more than 1% of my account on this trade?
	•	Is there a high-impact news event in the next 30 minutes?
	•	Am I in a calm, neutral emotional state right now?
	•	If this trade loses, will it put me in violation of my daily or weekly loss limit?
If the answer to any question is 'no' or 'uncertain', do not place the trade.
